How to Use Color Palettes in Your Design Projects

Color is one of the most powerful tools in a designer's toolkit. The right color palette can make your designs look professional, evoke the right emotions, and create visual harmony. Our Color Palette Extractor helps you discover color schemes from any image — a great way to build a cohesive palette from a photo, a brand logo, or a piece of art that you love.

Why Extract Colors from Images?

Creating a color palette from scratch can be intimidating, especially if you do not have a formal design background. By extracting colors from an existing image — a photograph of a sunset, a picture of your favorite product, or a screenshot of a website whose colors you admire — you get a ready-made palette that is guaranteed to work well together because nature and professional designers have already done the hard work. You can then use these colors in your own projects: website designs, social media graphics, presentations, branding materials, or digital art.

Understanding Dominant Colors

The Color Palette Extractor analyzes every pixel in your uploaded image and groups them by color similarity. It then identifies the most frequently occurring colors — these are the dominant colors. The tool presents them as clickable swatches with their exact hex codes. Hex codes are the standard way to specify colors in web design (for example, #6D72C3 is a medium purple). You can click any swatch to copy its hex code to your clipboard and paste it directly into CSS, design software like Figma or Adobe XD, or any color picker input.

Building a Complete Design System

Once you have your extracted palette, think about how to use each color. A common approach is to designate one color as the primary brand color (usually the most dominant or striking one), one as a secondary accent color, and the rest as supporting colors for backgrounds, borders, or subtle details. Aim for good contrast between text and background colors — a quick test is to view your palette in grayscale to ensure there is enough tonal variation. You can combine your extracted palette with neutral colors like white, black, or gray for a more balanced design system.

How to Use This Tool

Upload any image and the Color Palette Extractor will immediately analyze it and display the dominant colors. Click any color swatch to copy its hex code. Try uploading different types of images — landscapes, product shots, abstract art — to see how their palettes differ. Each image tells a unique color story.
